For m tree edges, the polygon has m+2 vertices. Its vertex numbers are polygon positions, not the tree's graceful labels. Root transfer: an illustrated glossary

The goal is to find a proof of the Graceful Tree Conjecture. Root transfer is a proposed way to make leaf-by-leaf extension possible.

1. Change the labeling, keep the tree

Root: the vertex where we want to attach a new leaf. It does not change the underlying tree.

Witness: a graceful labeling together with a valid extension cut and threshold at that root.

Root transfer: obtain some witness at v from the existence of a witness at its neighbor u. A single labeling need not work at both roots.

2. Open a space for a new leaf

Cut c: a horizontal line between label heights c−1 and c. An edge crosses it when its endpoint labels lie on opposite sides.

Threshold k: a cut works when its crossing edge lengths are exactly k, k+1, …, n−1. Shifting the upper labels increases just these lengths by one, leaving edge length k unused.

Port: a root whose original label is c−k or c+k−1. After the shift, attaching a leaf labeled c at that root supplies the missing length k.

Graceful labeling
Use vertex labels 0 through n−1 exactly once. The absolute differences along the edges must then be 1 through n−1 exactly once.
Edge length
The absolute difference of endpoint labels, not the length of the line on the screen.
Extendable at a root
There is a valid cut and port at that vertex, so the construction above gives a graceful labeling after adding a leaf there.
Common vs. separate witnesses
A common witness is one labeling extendable at both roots, possibly with different cuts. Separate witnesses use different labelings. Root transfer allows either.
Vertex-label and edge-length cycles
Bookkeeping for changes between two labelings on fixed vertices and edges. A cycle (a b c) means a becomes b, b becomes c, and c becomes a. These are permutation cycles, not loops in the tree; they do not by themselves prove a transfer rule.

The proposed transfer lemma: if a tree has a graceful labeling extendable at u, then it has some graceful labeling extendable at every neighbor v of u. This is the statement we want to prove, not an established result.

Why it would help: a graceful labeling is always extendable at its vertex labeled 0 using c=k=1. Transfer across adjacent edges would make every vertex available for leaf attachment, enabling an inductive proof starting with the two-vertex tree.
